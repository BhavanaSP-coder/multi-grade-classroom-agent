Subject: Mathematics
Grade: 3
Unit: Introduction to Fractions

Learning outcomes (demo)
- Understands a fraction as a part of a whole divided into equal parts: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 and simple fractions like 2/4, 3/4.
- Reads and writes fractions using numerator and denominator in simple language ("top number tells how many parts we take, bottom number tells how many equal parts in all").
- Represents fractions with drawings (shapes, number strips) and with groups of objects.
- Finds a fraction of a small collection, for example 1/3 of 9 marbles.
- Compares unit fractions with pictures: 1/2 is bigger than 1/4 when the wholes are the same.

Suggested low-resource activities (demo)
1. Paper strip fractions: each child folds a strip of paper into 2, 3 or 4 equal parts and labels each part (1/2, 1/3, 1/4). Compare strips side by side.
2. Fraction of a group: give 12 seeds. Make 3 equal groups. One group is 1/3 of 12. Record "1/3 of 12 = 4".
3. Blackboard shading: the teacher draws shapes; children come up and shade the given fraction.
4. Fraction hunt: in the class, what fraction of the children are wearing slippers? What fraction of the window panes are open?

Assessment ideas (demo)
- Write the fraction for the shaded part of each shape.
- Draw a circle and shade 3/4.
- Which is bigger, 1/2 or 1/3 of the same roti? Draw to explain.

Teacher notes (demo)
Always insist that the parts are equal. Introduce the words numerator and denominator gently; the meaning matters more than the names. Use the same size whole when comparing fractions.
